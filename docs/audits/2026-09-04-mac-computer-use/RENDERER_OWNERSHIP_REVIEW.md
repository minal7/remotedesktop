# Renderer ownership and submission readiness

Source review and repair, 2026-09-10. This continues the [pointer-boundary review](POINTER_BOUNDARY_REVIEW.md) and [content-placement review](CAPTURE_CONTENT_PLACEMENT_REVIEW.md). No builds, typechecks, XCTest/assertion execution, native rendering/capture, application launches, browser actions or model inference were run. The installed app remains unchanged.

## First principles and observed defects

Input should refer to what the person can currently see. A transport connection, decoded frame, renderer submission and actual presented pixels are distinct events. A callback from a separate observer cannot prove that the visible renderer received that frame. Local ownership can fence callbacks, but cannot invent evidence that a native renderer displayed current capture content.

Before this repair:

1. WebRTC delivered frames directly to the view's renderer and independently to `FirstVideoFrameProbe`. The probe could report readiness without the real sink having been attached or called.
2. Attaching another sink overwrote the saved renderer and added the new one without removing the former registration. Cleanup used the current track, losing the exact track/sink pair that originally needed removal.
3. The Computer Use screen created `RemoteScreenView` only after interactive readiness. Its sidecar also refused renderer attachment until live. Making the renderer authoritative without changing both conditions would create a circular wait.
4. The view's binding followed the `SessionModel` object, while the session and sidecar could replace their transport internally. An unchanged view could remain attached to the old target or miss the replacement.
5. There was no frame-only readiness loss callback. Full input invalidation clears the hello and display, which is appropriate for revoked host input but insufficient for replacement of a local renderer under a still-valid handshake.

## Source repair

[RemoteVideoRendererBinding](../../../ios/RemoteDesktop/Transport/RemoteVideoRendererBinding.swift) retains one desired sink and target, plus the exact installed pair. It detaches the previous pair before installing a successor. Repeating the same attachment is idempotent, and cleanup for a displaced sink cannot detach its replacement. The desired sink survives a period with no transport. Reentrant target callbacks update desired state; reconciliation reads that latest state after the admitted operation returns.

[SessionModel](../../../ios/RemoteDesktop/SessionModel.swift) owns this binding and follows its current ordinary transport or local Computer Use sidecar. The sidecar owns another binding to its current video transport. Both can prepare a renderer before media readiness. Sidecar callbacks weakly retain their transport and require it to remain the exact current target. Retirement/replacement checks its attempt generation after external callbacks, and a local presentation ID distinguishes replacement hellos within one connection attempt.

`RemoteVideoRenderSubscription` is a native renderer adapter with a synchronous admission gate. Each track registration gets a new instance. It forwards valid size callbacks and valid nonnil decoded frames to its downstream sink. Only a successfully returned frame call can trigger its one-shot submission event; size-only and nil-frame callbacks cannot. Retirement closes admission, joins an already admitted synchronous sink call, and prevents later callbacks from entering that sink. Reentrant retirement from the sink suppresses readiness for the admitted call. The callback runs outside the gate.

[WebRTCTransport](../../../ios/RemoteDesktop/Transport/WebRTCTransport.swift) retains the exact track, subscription and input-presentation lease. It installs only after the current hello and matching display, with both a desired sink and video track. Replacement first removes the registration from local ownership, retires its gate, then removes it from its retained original track. The queued MainActor submission callback checks the current track and lease. Nested renderer updates defer installation until the outer update finishes, preventing a cleanup callback from installing a captured predecessor. Raw views are no longer added directly to the native video track.

The new `onVideoFrameUnavailable` [transport callback](../../../ios/RemoteDesktop/Transport/Transport.swift) withdraws submission readiness without revoking the host handshake. The model releases its existing controls under their original input scope before clearing local sources and rotating their generation. Reentrant teardown cannot then clear a successor generation. Ordinary remote control retains its hello/display. The sidecar retains its own compatible hello and display, waits for another renderer submission, and republishes that layout before going live. Full `onInputUnavailable` still clears the handshake and requires recovery negotiation.

[ComputerUseView](../../../ios/RemoteDesktop/ComputerUse/ComputerUseView.swift) now creates the renderer when its screen transport is preparing, independently of interactive readiness. It remains mounted behind the opaque waiting overlay during transient frame loss. Hit testing and accessibility stay disabled while unavailable or privacy-shielded. The unreachable second waiting panel is removed, and the waiting label no longer calls an unproven frame “fresh.” The historical `.waitingForFreshFrame` enum case remains; its name is not freshness evidence.

## Remaining repair and qualification sequence

1. **Own the renderer's deferred work and presented frame.** This gate controls entry into the sink, not work the sink schedules internally. An admitted old `setSize` or `renderFrame` may have deferred effects after retirement. Qualify the pinned renderer's threading and add an owned presentation acknowledgement tied to the exact layout/attachment. Do not equate a returned call with a Metal drawable being presented. Sending nil is not a verified way to clear cached pixels.
2. **Carry source identity through the actual media path.** A new subscription may still receive a queued old decode. Bind source capture identity through adaptation, encode, transport, decode and the owning renderer. Independent data-channel metadata or packet timestamps alone do not establish resulting pixel identity. Follow the [media evidence design](CAPTURE_FRESH_IMAGE_DESIGN_REVIEW.md).
3. **Make retirement bounded without abandoning native ownership.** The synchronous sink gate can delay retirement if an admitted sink blocks. The sink must not synchronously wait for MainActor while retirement waits for that sink. Qualify actual native add/remove/decode/size/render ordering and failure behavior before changing the gate or relying on it for responsiveness. The serial fixtures do not prove concurrency or native lock ordering.
4. **Recover stationary screens and foreground return.** Add bounded owned fresh-image requests, real-operation capacity accounting, qualified timestamps and stream continuity. Rearm presentation on activity return; old cached pixels are insufficient. Silent stalls and unsupported frame output need explicit liveness policy. Keep the [capture recovery bounds](CAPTURE_RECOVERY_BUDGET_REVIEW.md).
5. **Verify native layout and input together.** Exercise attachment replacement, background/foreground, account shielding, rotation/zoom, queued size changes, stream replacement and display changes while input is held. Independently observe the actual pixels, target, delivered text and release events; pure geometry or callbacks are insufficient.
6. **Qualify the exact paused login task.** Preserve its identity and TLS controls through screen loss/replacement. Resume only through the existing explicit handback and fresh account/page checks. Closing a popup does not prove successful authentication. This repair neither completes a login nor enables autonomous credential handling.
7. **Run the deferred whole-task scenarios only after permission.** Actual travel, concert and movie searches must use the app's planner/actions and independent result verification. Model substitutions or RTX3080 training require measured failures and held-out evaluation; 8 GB and thermal qualification remain later work. None of those gates is closed by this source repair.
