Remote Desktop

Remote Desktop lets you see and control your Mac or Windows computer from your iPhone or iPad. Computer use (Beta) lets you ask a supported Mac to carry out a task locally. Final smoke, safety, and physical iPhone or iPad acceptance results are pending.

Need help or found a bug?

Open an issue on our GitHub support page and we'll get back to you. Please include your device model, iOS version, and whether your computer is a Mac or Windows PC.

Getting started

  1. On your Mac or Windows computer, download and install the free host app from our downloads page.
  2. Sign the computer in to the same Apple Account as your iPhone or iPad.
  3. Open the host app and leave it listening.
  4. Open Remote Desktop on your device. Your computer appears automatically; choose it to connect.

Set up Computer use (Beta) on a Mac

  1. Use an Apple silicon Mac with at least 16 GB of memory. Keep about 4.3 GiB of free disk space for Fara 1.5-4B setup; free disk space if setup reports that there is not enough.
  2. In Remote Desktop on your iPhone or iPad, select the Mac and start Computer use setup. Follow the on-screen progress. Setup verifies downloaded model files and can resume an interrupted download.
  3. On the Mac, allow Screen Recording and Accessibility for Remote Desktop Host when macOS asks. These permissions let the host observe the screen and perform the actions you request.
  4. Keep the iPhone and Mac on the same local network for Computer use tasks. The Mac may connect over Wi-Fi or Ethernet. After enrollment, task prompts, status, results, and approvals use the authenticated local connection to the Mac.

Computer use runs Fara 1.5-4B on the Mac. It can narrate progress, ask a question, request approval before a consequential action, or stop and ask for help. The feature is in beta; final release-candidate measurements and physical iPhone or iPad checks are pending.

Example tasks

Try a small task such as “Open Calculator and calculate 17 times 23” or “Read the heading and first paragraph on the page that is already open.” Avoid entering passwords or other credentials. Use test content for any task that could change or submit information.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't my device find my computer?

Both devices must be signed in to the same Apple Account. Double-check the iCloud account on each, make sure the host app is open, and keep both devices online while automatic pairing finishes.

My computer says it is ready, but the device is still pairing.

Keep the host open and confirm both devices use the same Apple Account. Automatic pairing uses the account's private CloudKit database and normally finishes without any code or copied secret. Restarting the host refreshes its private advertisement if it became stale.

The connection won't establish.

For the most reliable connection, put your device and computer on the same local network. The Mac can use Wi-Fi or Ethernet. Some restrictive or public networks block the direct connection the app needs.

Does an AI task go through iCloud?

No. CloudKit can help your devices discover and enroll each other, negotiate a remote screen, and report AI component setup progress. After enrollment, prompts, conversation, task status and results, controls, and approvals use only the authenticated local-network connection to your Mac. If that secure local channel is unavailable, the task stops instead of falling back to CloudKit. Screen pixels and direct input use the separate WebRTC connection, while planning and visual grounding stay on the Mac.

Why did Computer use stop or ask a question?

The model may need missing information, may be unable to identify a safe next action, or may reach a permission or system dialog. Answer the question or close the dialog on the Mac, then resume if the app offers that option. You can also stop or take over the live Mac view. Do not enter passwords or other credentials into a task.

What permissions does the host app need?

On macOS, the host app needs Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions so it can share the screen and accept your input. Windows uses the built-in screen capture and input features.

A Siri or Dictation overlay appears during a Mac task.

You do not need to turn off Siri or Apple Intelligence. If the overlay does not block the task, leave it alone. If it covers a control or the task pauses, close the overlay on the Mac and choose Resume. Some macOS versions offer a separate Dictation keyboard shortcut; changing that shortcut is optional. See the Privacy Policy for how local task history is stored and deleted.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no separate Remote Desktop account, subscription, or fee. Pairing does use the Apple Account already signed in to iCloud on both devices.

Is it private?

Yes — your screen, audio, and input stream directly between your own devices, and pairing only works within your own Apple Account. See our Privacy Policy for details.
